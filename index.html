How to read the moves

LETTEREach letter is a face: Front, Back, Up, Down, Left, Right. Turn only that layer 90°, clockwise as you look straight at it.
PRIMEA letter with an apostrophe, like R', means turn that same layer counter‑clockwise instead.
2A letter followed by 2, like U2, means turn that layer a full 180° — direction doesn't matter.
ORDERMoves always run left‑to‑right. Never skip one or do them out of sequence — each step sets up the next.
◆

Before You Start — Cube Basics

PIECESA cube has three kinds of piece. Centers (1 sticker) never move relative to each other and tell you what color each side must become. Edges (2 stickers) sit between two centers. Corners (3 stickers) sit at the corners. You'll only ever be moving edges and corners — the centers are your fixed map.
DEFAULT HOLDUnless a stage says otherwise, hold the cube with white on the bottom, yellow on top, and keep it that way — don't spin the whole cube around to "get a better angle" mid-stage, or you'll lose track of which layer is which.
TWO HANDS, ONE JOBOne hand anchors the cube and barely moves. The other hand (or its thumb/index finger) does the actual turning. If both hands are moving at once, you've probably rotated the whole cube by accident.
DON'T FORCE ITIf a stage's starting picture doesn't look like your cube, that's fine — it's just one example case. Re-read the "Hold it like this" box, match your cube's actual pattern to it as closely as you can, and the same moves will still get you there.

Finger Tricks — Turn Faster Without Regripping

Once you can execute every stage above without checking the sequence, speed stops being about knowing the moves and starts being about how your fingers make them. Try each move on the practice cube below, then use the reference grid any time you want a reminder — grips vary a bit from person to person, so treat these as a solid starting point rather than a strict rule.

Tap a move above to see the recommended fingertrick and watch it played on the cube.
GRIPHold the cube loosely with fingertips near chest height, not in your palms — a tight grip is the single biggest speed killer.
NO REGRIPTry to trigger the next move without letting go of the cube between turns. That habit alone is most of what "speed" actually is.
LOOK AHEADWhile your hands finish the current move, your eyes should already be searching for the next piece.
SLOW → FASTDrill a new algorithm slowly with clean fingertricks first. Speed comes from repetition, never from rushing early.
Practice each stage until it feels automatic before moving to the next — muscle memory beats memorizing.
